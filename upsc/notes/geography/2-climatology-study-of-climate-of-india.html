  • Monsoon

    Introduction

    • Introduction

      1. Indian Climate is described as Tropical Monsoon Climate
      2. Monsoon is derived from Arabic Word Mausim meaning Season
      3. Rig Veda mentions Monsoon
      4. IN 3rd BC Hippatus from Greek Mentions Monsoon
      5. Arab Geo coined it for the first time, Monsoon has been documented in Rig Veda also
      6. Alberuni brought it to Limelight. Experience of Gujarat Coast
      7. Monsoon is a Seasonal Wind, Periodic Winds
      8. It Indicates Seasonal Changes, in the distribution and pattern of Rainfall, Temperature, Atmospheric Pressure and direction of winds on the basis of seasonal distribution of above 4 Parameters
      9. On the Basis of above 4 parameters Climate of India is divided into the following
        1. Winter - Cold and Dry - Dec Jan Feb
        2. Summer - Hot & Dry - Apr & May
        3. SW Monsoon - Hot & Humid - June July Aug Sep
        4. Retreating Monsoon / October Heat - Oct Nov
      10. In Spite of the fact that Great Plains of India are located in Subtropical Zones but still Indian Climate is called as Tropical Indicating it is Hot and Humid Conditions with Seasonal Rainfall
      11. Monsoons are Modification of Trade Winds. Hence also called as Cross Equatorial Westerlies

    • Tropicality of India has been Contributed by the following Physiographic Factors

      1. Himalayan Mountains - Which Lie to the North of Great Plains

      2. Indian Peninsula surrounded by Sea from all three sides

        Range of Temp = Continentality = More as we move away from Coast

      3. In Winter Season Extremely Cold Polar winds are prevented by the Himalayan Mountain Ranges to flow in the Great Plains of India

        Here Siberian Extremely Cold Winds are blocked by Himalayas

    Air Mass Concept of Monsoon

    • Conditions Required for Formation of an Air Mass

      1. Air Mass Becomes Stationary → When Kinetic Energy of Wind becomes Equal to Frictional Force of Wind the Air Mass becomes Stationary

      2. Isotrophic Surface on Land or Ocean

      3. Hill or Mountainous Topography → Rugged Surface should not be Present → Rolling Plains are suitable for creation of Air Mass

        Because of Hills or Mountains, The Hills tend to deform the Creation Air Mass

      4. Upper Atmospheric and Surface Conditions

        In the Upper Atmosphere - Cyclonic Conditions - LP

        Surface - Anticyclonic Conditions - HP

    Details of Indian Monsoon

    • Basics of Monsoon

      1. Monsoon is a Secondary Circulation, Produced by Thermodynamic Modification with Trade Winds over Indian Sub Continent due to apparent shifting of sun across the seasons.
      2. It is also defined as the Seasonal Reversal of Wind Systems
      3. it is characterised by Rhythm of Seasons i.e Summer and Winter ; Dry and Wet Spell of Rainfall and Summer Concentration of Rainfall (Max Rainfall in Summers)

    • Features of Indian Monsoon

      1. If Deflection//Reversal of Wind is more than 120 degree out of Max 180 degree is called Monsoon
      2. If Deflection of Wind is less than 120 degree out of 180 degree is called Pseudo Monsoon
        1. In Month of July Increase in Frequency of Wind by 40% Occurs because of Increased Pressure Gradient
      1. In July Acceleration of Wind is by 3 cm per square min ( 3 cm/min2)
        1. Rhythm of Season and Reversal of Hadley Cell

        Earlier Hadley cell which was from Equator to Tropic is Now from Tropic to Equator

        1. Cross Equatorial Oceanic Winds (Cross Equatorial South Westerlies) and Continental Winds

    • Role of Tropical Easterly and Tibetan Plateau in Monsoon - Class Dictation

      1. In the Year 1960’s Professor Koteswaram Revolutionised the Monsoon Theory by Introducing

        1. Role of Tibetan Plateau
        2. Role of Tropical Easterly Jet

      2. His Theory is Known as Jet Stream Theory, Between 1973 and 78

      3. Monsoon Expedition was Launched by Indo Russian Scientist Group Jointly

      4. The Monext Theory Added Several New Dimensions to the Monsoon Mechanism and our Understanding of Monsoon, Further Grew with the Study of El Nino

        Therefore Presently a Synthetic Approach is adopted to Explain Monsoon

      5. The Heating of Tibetan Plateau because of Its altitude (Avg Height 4 KM) and receives sun rays at lesser distance (More Flux of Insolation)

      6. Tibetan Plateau is Barren with presence of rocks. Rocks have Low Albedo (Absorb More Heat). Hence due to Rocks, It becomes heat sink Zone.

      7. Due to Heating, the Air Tends to Rise upto 12 KM in the Atmosphere, thus resulting in High Pressure and Creation of Anticyclonic Conditions

      8. This Anticyclonic Conditions Results into Divergence or Out Flow of Air

      9. The Subtropical Jet Stream meets with This Anticyclone and Forms of Vortex

        And one of the Limb enter the Subtropical Jet and while one of the Limb Drift over North Eastern India and become Tropical Easterly Jet due to Coriolis and Start Crossing Over Karnataka and Begin to Sink over Mascarene Island (East of Madagascar)

        It Intensifies Already Intensified HP created over Mascarene Island due to Increased Pressure Gradient between Two Places and Sinking of Jet Stream over HP Area

    • Retreat of Monsoon (Retreat and North East Monsoon)

      • True Retreating Monsoon

        1. Retreat is Gradual which begins from 2st of september to 30th November. It is caused by the following factors. Southward migration of sun and shifting of ITCZ
        2. Diminishing Low Pressure System over Tibetan Plateau and Disappearance of Tropical Easterly Jet
        3. During this time, Himalayas bifurcate the Subtropical Jet into Two Branches. One flows to the south of Himalayas and one North of It

        • Trough and Ridge of Jet Stream Air - Concept

          1. Trough - Convergence
          2. Ridge - Divergence

        • Application of Above Concept to India

          Sub Tropical Trough is Bifurcated by the Height of Himalayas into Two Branches, The Trough of the Jet is Located to the North West of India in Mainly Rajasthan

          Thus Below the Trough of the Jet, Anti Cyclonic Flows are Maintained which causes the Retreat.

          The Monsoon Retreat, bring 10 to 15 CM Rainfall from Available Moisture in the Soil

      • Retreat Monsoon and North Eastern Monsoon - Combined

        1. Retreat Monsoon and North East Monsoon is NOT Same
          1. Retreat is on Continent
          2. North East Monsoon is on Ocean or Sea

      • North Eastern Monsoon - Extension of Retreating Monsoon

        When the Retreat Monsoon Moves Over Bay of Bengal it picks up Sea from Moisture and becomes North Easterlies due to Coriolis Force

    • Western Disturbance

  • El Nino & La Nina ( Allied Walker Cell Reversal)

    Important Point to Note

    1. Wind Moves from High to Low
    2. Warm Place - Water or Air - High Pressure
    3. Cold Place - Water or Air - Low Pressure
    4. Sinking Air does not give Rainfall as it Suppresses Clouds → Leading to Dryness and Sometimes Forest Fires in Australia
    5. Wind Diverges from High Pressure
    6. The Air Which Rises becomes Cold

  • Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)

    It is called Dipole because two places are into consideration

    IOD is also called India NINA as well. It means EL Nino like phenonmenon in Indian Ocean

    IOD is a Local Phenonmenon confined to Indian Ocean

    It can happen at any time but if it happens during Monsoon then its a issue based on positive or negative

    Earlier it was occuring in every 5 to 7 years but now the phenomenon is frequent and hence we can say that the sea surface temperature is increasing

    One of the reasons being given is the Nuclear Tests done by USA in Funafuti Island of Marhsall Islands under USA

    Another Reason given there is Solar Cycle (Phase of 7 to 13 Years) and Sun Spot Activities. But now Solar Cycle Phenomenon has become a more freuqent Option

    Another Reason given is that of Aphelion and Perihilion, but the reasons does not seem to be that strong as this is also dependent on the Clarity of Sky

    • Positive IOD → Good Monsoon

      1. Western Indian Ocean → Somalia → Low Pressure → Temp High → Downwelling Warm Water

      2. Eastern Indian Ocean → Indonesia → High Pressure → Temp Low → Upwelling of Cold Water

      3. Thermocline will be created

      4. Monsoon will be good for India because South East Trade Winds of Arabian Sea Branch will carry moisture from the IOD Movemenets of Winds Sinking in Bay of Bengal

      5. Frequency of Tropical Cyclone in Arabian Sea will Increase

        Frequency of Tropial Cyclone in Bay of Bengal will Decrease

        For Tropical Cyclone, we need 27 degree celsius temperature and warm water at the depth of atleast 60 Meter

      6. IOD nullifies effect of El Nino

      7. Australlia, Borneo Island will experience Forest Fires because of HP created by sinking of Dry Air of Positive IOD and El Nino

    • Negative IOD → Bad Monsoon

      1. Western Indian Ocean → Somalia → High Pressure → Temp Low
      2. Eastern Indian Ocean → Indonesia → Low Pressure → Temp High
      3. Monsoon will be bad for India
