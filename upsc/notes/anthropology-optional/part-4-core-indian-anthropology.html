    • Syllabus

    • A Basic Timeline

      1. Emergence of anthropology in India is a result of British Administration Policies in India. British studied Indians in order to be able to administer India better for her diversity in cultures, people, terrains, etc

      2. 1774: Asiatic Society was established

      3. 1784: Transformed into Asiatic Society of Bengal (William Jones) This society began publishing a Journal

      4. 1793: This Journal began dedicating a particular part of it for anthropological studies

      5. 1807: The Governor General in 1807 appointed Dr Francis Buchanan as the Head of Studies related to Indian Population, specifically for Bengal to find out its society and culture. His studies were one of the significant reasons for Raja Ram Mohan Roy to find out about social evils in India.

      6. 1886: The First dedicated anthropological journal was from the anthropological society of Bombay in 1886. This era laid down the stone of foundation of anthropology in India

      7. 1850+ : In the second half of 19th century, British anthropologists were called to India to study Indian society and culture

        This motivated Indians to take part in such anthropological studies and take interest in studying about Indian populations and cultures

    • Phases of Anthropology in India: (Given by Surajit Sinha and LP Vidyarthi)

      • Formative Phase (1774-1919)
        1. Asiatic Society
        2. Asiatic Society of Bengal + Journal
        3. Part III of Anthropological Society of Bombay
        4. 1807 Dr. Francis
        5. Various British Researchers → Hutton, Risley, Perry, Thurston & Russell
        6. True Anthropologists → Hutton, WHR Rivers, Radcliffe Brown, Seligman
        7. Contribution of Risley
          1. gave multiple monographs on tribes and castes of India. Impressed by his work, he was appointed as the head of population studies in India
          2. 1881: 1st regular census of India was conducted
          3. 1901: Risley conducted a census
          4. Risley’s most important work “The People of India”. Many Indian anthropologists consider this publication of true anthropology in India. The most prominent supporter of this is Gopal Sarana
        8. Journal of anthropological society of Bombay in 1886.
        9. EB Tylor, James Frazer & Hugsley were made honorary members of this society
        10. In the last years of this phase, we started witnessing entry of discussions of anthropology as a discipline of study in universities.
        11. In 1919, 1st section of anthropology was created under the department of sociology in university of Bombay
        12. Grierson's linguistic divisions of India
        13. Topics studied during this phase – study of tribes, study of caste and culture and study of natural history
        14. According to DN Majumdar this phase ended in 1911.
      • Constructive Phase (1920-1949)
        1. The 1st dedicated of department of anthropology at university of Calcutta. This was offered under post graduate studies in India. The earlier batches from this university had pioneers like NK Bose, DN Majumdar, Dharmi Sen, TC Das, Andre Beteille
        2. In 1947, University of Delhi, a department of anthropology was created. This led to spread of anthropology in different universities across the country like 1950 Lucknow, 1952 Guwahati
        3. Batch of anthropologists who studied outside of India like GS Ghurrey, Chattopadhyaya (both are students of WHR Rivers), Dr Irawati Karve (graduated, post graduate and PhD from Germany ; famous for kinship studies in India)
        4. Indian anthropologists were mainly supportive of functionalism, diffusionism and evolutionism
        5. In late 1930s, there was huge shift in Indian anthropology when Verrier Elwin began working on tribes of India. He mentioned that tribes should not be disturbed at all. Let them be incorporated & assimilated. He propagated for them to stay isolated → “Isolation” of Elwin
        6. This began the Ghurrey vs Elwin debate. Where Ghurrey did not want them to be isolated and was supportive of assimilative approach. In this debate, there was opinions given by Hutton that tribes were backward Hindus and was supported by Ghurrey.
        7. This gave rise to the Tribal Panchsheel.
      • Analytic Phase (1950-1990)
        1. Tilt from focus of anthropological studies from tribes to villages and caste system post-independence
        2. Impact of Industrialization, Urbanization & Globalization which led to shift from pre-literate villages to complex villages in India
        3. American anthropologists studied villages. This became immortal in the anthropological studies of India like
          1. Madhopur Village & Rampur Village studied by → Moris Oppler
          2. Rampura Village was Studied By → Andre Beteille & Oscar Lewis
          3. Karimpur Village → Wiser & Wiser
        4. Some of these villages are further studied by Indian Anthropologists like
          1. Rampura village by → MN Srinivas
          2. Mohana Village (Near Lucknow) → DN Majumdar
          3. Haveli Taluka by → GS Ghurrey & Professor Yogendra Singh
          4. Shamirpet by → SC Dubey
          5. Sripuram Village, TN → Andre Beteille
        5. This led to emergence of applied and action anthropology. When so many studies were being conducting in different parts of the country, it helped anthropologists to raise issues and problems of the people studied, usually the oppressed classes or tribals
        6. PK Bhowmick studied the villages of Lodhas (Vidisha Village), he found that based on their system of existence, we should create a society for propagation, improvement of their way of life.
        7. This led to the creation of Institute of Social Research and Applied Anthropology – First institution created for local people after anthropological studies
        8. In 1960s and 1970s, we witnessed a boom in anthropological studies in different parts of the country
        9. SC Dubey criticized the government for not using anthropological studies and research for formulating plans and framing policies to help the people of the country
        10. At this point, there was lack of funds for Indian anthropologists
      • Evaluative Phase(1990–Present)
        1. Western Anthropologists were complaining about the complexity of Indian society and culture, which made it difficult to study. To which Indian anthropologists responded saying that this is the reason why they exist. That you need an Indian approach to studying Indian people and societies. So, this period witnessed a increase in the use of blend of a western and Indian approach to anthropological studies
        2. Some Indian anthropologists tried to give modified version of theories given by western anthropologists
        3. This era witnessed an increase in applied anthropology in sports, health and medicine, better understanding of social aspects of diseases, biological basis for races, etc
        4. Increase in awareness
          1. Bauxite mine Niyamgiri hills Odisha,
          2. Tata plant WB

    • Indian Villages & Significance of Village Studies

      1. Origin of Indian village can be traced far back in history with the timeline matching with history of civilization in India
      2. In arthashastra, kautilya mentioned that it is the duty of every ruler or the king to maintain the administrative affairs of villages so that a stability and balance can be maintained
      3. In medieval times kitab-al-hind(11 cent) by Al Baruni ,gives an account of caste occupations and subsequent bases for organization of villages British colonial perspective related to India was guided mainly by villages and the majority view was that Indian villages are independent and self sufficient units
      4. James Mill and Charles Metcalf considered India as land of villages
      5. 1801: Tagged Indian villages as Little republics with as social harmony and economic balance based on intra and inter caste relationships and jajmani system. Thomas Monro he also referred them as mini republics
      6. The social harmony and economic balance due to division of labour provided a practical functionality to village as a unit and is a self sufficient entity
      7. Multiple civilizations ,rulers ,disasters happened from time to time but village as a unit survived in India
      8. Late 19 cent and early 20th cent witnessed emergence of number of village studies to figure out the social transformations and also to study the divisiveness ,inequality and operation of lower caste in a village system
      9. In the words of Gandhiji ‘soul of India lives in its villages’ and if a representative of Indianness has to be visualized it can be reflected in India’s villages only
      10. Gandhiji was in favour of self sustained ,self ruled villages i.e gram swaraj through promoting the panchayats
      11. Dr. BR Ambedkar was of the view that Indian village is a cesspool (collection) of factionalism and then of inequality due to prevalent caste system. Were the lower caste are being suppressed in all the aspects of daily life
