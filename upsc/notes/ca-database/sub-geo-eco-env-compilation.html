  • COP 28

    General Overview:

    • Initiation: Began in 1995 as the United Nations Conference of Parties.
    • Language Importance: Unanimity required; every line is critically agreed upon.
    • UNFCC Inception: Came into force in 1994, distinguishing between Annex (developed) & Non-Annex (developing) countries.

    Key Focus Areas:

    • Renewable Energy: Aim to triple global capacity.
    • Global Stocktake: First 5-yearly review under the 2015 Paris Agreement; first in 2023.
    • India's Position: No commitment to specific COP 28 goals.
    • Loss and Damage Fund: Established at the World Bank with initial $250 million; targets $1.5 trillion.
    • Methane Reduction: US & China pledge to cut emissions.
    • Innovation Example: Masdar City, UAE - a zero-carbon initiative.

    COP 26 Insights:

    • India's Advocacy: Preferred 'phasing down' over 'phasing out' coal and fossil fuels.
    • Renewable Targets: Influenced subsequent COP 28 discussions and goals.

    Climate Finance:

    • Definition: Financial flows to combat climate change.
    • Estimates: OECD at $88 billion, Oxfam at around $22 billion.

    Additional Points:

    • Global Temperature Rise: 1.1°C above pre-industrial levels.
    • Adaptation Goals: Linked to COP 21's global adaptation goal.
    • Germany's Funding Ruling: €60 billion for COVID-19 fund not for climate.
    • India's Commitments: Reduce emissions by 1 billion tons by 2030; net-zero by 2070.

    COP 28 Specifics:

    • Temperature Goal: Keep global increase below 1.5°C, aligning with IPCC suggestions.
    • Coal Debate: Divergent views on 'phase out' by major users like India, Indonesia, and China.
    • Emission Reduction: Target 43% reduction by 2030 from 2019 levels & 60% by 2045
    • Dubai Consensus: Shift towards transitioning away from fossil fuels.
    • Signatories: 198 countries participating in COP 28.
    • Methane Focus: Addressing its potent heat-trapping ability.
    • Natural Gas: Considered a transitional energy source.

    Technologies:

    • CCS & CDR: Capture and removal technologies for carbon dioxide.
    • IPCC Report: Focuses on climate mitigation strategies.
    • Overshoot Scenario: Temporary exceeding of temperature limits.
    • Land Use for CO2 Removal: Proposals to use extensive land for carbon capture.
    • BECCS: Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage as a CDR method.
    • Transition Costs: High for CDR, reliant on fossil fuels for CCR.

    India Specific:

    • Import Dependence: High for fossil fuels and gas.
    • Incentives: PLI for solar and battery technology.
    • Authority: National Disaster Management to operationalize Loss and Damage Fund.
    • CCUR & CBDR: Concepts defining responsibilities and carbon capture/utilization strategie

    2. Cities Consume 75% of primary energy responsible for a 70% of carbon dioxide emission
    3. Nothing for Us Without Us - Slogan
    4. Cities Excluded From → Nationally Determined Contributions and National Adapted Plans
