  • Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002

    • The Prevention of Money Laundering Act (PMLA), 2002 is an Indian law enacted in 2002 to prevent money laundering and related offenses.
      • Money laundering involves making illegally obtained funds appear legitimate or "clean" by integrating them into the financial system. Hence, statement 2 is correct.
    • It was enacted in a 2002 response to India’s global commitment (including the Vienna Convention) to combat the menace of money laundering. Hence, statement 1 is correct.
    • The Directorate of Enforcement (ED) is the primary authority responsible for investigating and prosecuting money laundering.
      • It functions under the Department of Revenue of the Ministry of Finance.
      • Recently, the Supreme Court has ruled that the ED cannot arrest someone under the PMLA simply for not responding to their queries and summons. Hence, statement 3 is correct.

    • Key Sections: 13, 18, and 40 3D5 of UAPA.
    • Cases: Fahad Shah UAPA case & Vernon Gonsalves Case.
    • Section 16: Specifies punishments related to terrorist acts.
    • Associated Acts: Arms Act 1959, Foreigner Act 1946, Passport Act 1967, Citizenship Act 1955.
    • Section 38(2): Punishment for being a member of a terrorist organization.

  • Jammu & Kashmir Reorganisation Act (J&K RA), 2019

    1. Ladakh has Shia Majroity which wants to maintain relations with Valley
    2. Solution
      1. Disarmament and Demilitrisation of Area
      2. Soft Border Economy

    Background

    • Article 370: A form of asymmetric federalism and a temporary provision that granted special status to Jammu and Kashmir, allowing it internal sovereignty and special citizenship rights.
    • Article 35A: Worked in tandem with Article 370 to define the residents of the state and their special privileges.
    • Dissolution of Jammu and Kashmir Assembly: Occurred in 1957.

    Various Commentaries

    • Supreme Court's Stance: Upheld the validity of the abrogation of both Article 370 and Article 35A, indicating judicial deference.
    • President's Decision: Supported the removal of Article 370.
    • Pakistan's Response: Contends that the Supreme Court's decision has no legal value, planning to counter it via the UNSC Council.
    • RSS's Involvement: Passed 27 resolutions since 1950 advocating for the removal of Article 370.
    • Atal Bihari Vajpayee's Slogan: "Insaniyat, Jammu and Kashmir, and Kashmiriyat" aimed at promoting harmony in the region.

    Pros of Abrogation

    • Integration with Union: The Supreme Court suggested that permanent status of Article 370 would hinder the complete integration of Jammu and Kashmir with the rest of India.
    • Statehood and Elections: The proposal for the return of statehood to Jammu and Kashmir and conducting elections by September 30, 2024, can be seen as steps towards normalcy.
    • Vacant Seats in Local Bodies: The point about 12,000 vacant seats since 2018 indicates a need for political restructuring and representation.
    • Ladakh as Union Territory: The decision to make Ladakh a Union Territory is seen as a positive step for its development and governance.

    Cons of Abrogation

    • Loss of Special Status: The removal of Article 370 eliminates the unique status and autonomy that Jammu and Kashmir previously enjoyed.
    • Regional Tensions: The decision has been a point of contention between India and Pakistan, potentially escalating regional tensions.
    • Internal Conflict: The abrogation could lead to dissatisfaction and unrest among the residents of Jammu and Kashmir.

  • NCRB Data on Women
    • NCRB Data on Women: According to the Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA) in February 2023, domestic violence by husbands and relatives continues to be a major concern, even though overall crimes against women have decreased.
    • Women in Police Force: As of January 1, 2022, women constitute 11.7% of the total state police force.
    • Key Laws for Women’s Safety in India:
      1. The Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956: Aims to prevent trafficking for commercial sexual exploitation.
      2. The Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961: Prohibits the giving or receiving of a dowry as part of the marriage process.
      3. The Commission of Sati (Prevention) Act, 1987: Outlaws the practice of Sati, in which a widow immolates herself on her husband’s funeral pyre.
      4. Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005: Protects women from domestic violence, including physical, emotional, sexual, and economic abuse.
      5. The Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013: Addresses sexual harassment of women at the workplace.
      6. The Indecent Representation of Women (Prohibition) Act, 1986: Prohibits indecent representation of women through advertisements or in publications, writings, paintings, figures, or in any other manner.

  • Chief Election Commissioner Bill (CEC), 2023
    • Title: Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners (Appointment, Conditions of Service and Term of Office) Bill.
    • Background: Modification from the 1991 Act.
    • Appointment: ECs appointed by the President based on the recommendation of a selection committee.
    • Selection Committee Composition: Prime Minister (Chairperson), Leader of Opposition in the Lok Sabha, a Union Cabinet Minister nominated by the Prime Minister.
    • From Pool of : Secreatry of Central Gvoernment Bureaucrats
    • Salary and Conditions: Equivalent to the Cabinet Secretary, previously similar to a Supreme Court Judge.
    • Issues:
      • Lack of tenure security for ECs.
      • No set qualification criteria.
      • Unclear division of power between CEC and ECs.
      • Concerns about post-retirement appointments.
    • Dinesh Goswami Committee Reccomended : appointment of chief election Commissioner should be done by a committee which consist of the Prime Minister chief Justice of India and the leader of a position of Lok Sabha and the same was retreated by the Supreme Court under power of article 142
