    • JN 1 Flip Mutation

      • Omicron subvariant JN.1 is set to become dominant globally, carrying a "FLip" mutation (L455S) in its spike protein.
      • Despite dominance, a dominant strain isn't inherently more dangerous; viruses naturally mutate.
      • JN.1 evolved from the BA.2.86 lineage, distinct from other Omicron sublineages.
      • "FLip" mutations (L455S, L455F) increase transmissibility by altering amino acids on the spike protein.
      • JN.1 rapidly became predominant in France, overtaking other strains.
      • The mutation affects the virus's binding to ACE2 receptors, crucial for entry into human cells.
      • Enhanced binding reduces protection from natural antibodies, raising concerns about vaccine efficacy.
      • As of late 2023, JN.1 shows higher transmissibility and potential immune evasion.
      • Despite the surge in cases, no immediate need for additional vaccine doses for most individuals.
      • Accurate infection rates are challenging due to reduced testing and reporting.
      • JN.1 accounts for a significant portion of recent COVID-19 cases globally, indicating rapid spread.

      1. eSOIL

      eSoil is a low-power bioelectronic growth substrate that can electrically stimulate the root system and growth environment of plants in hydroponic environments.This novel substrate is environmentally friendly, being derived from cellulose and a conductive polymer called PEDOT. It offers a low energy, safe alternative to previous methods that required high voltage and non-biodegradable materials.In hydroponics, plants are grown without soil, requiring only water, nutrients and a substrate – something for their roots to attach to. This closed system allows water to be recirculated, ensuring that each seedling receives exactly the nutrients it needs. As a result, very little water is used and all the nutrients remain in the system – something that is not possible with conventional farming.

  • Banned FDC Drugs

    • Topic: Banned FDC Drug Use Case Report
    • Source: Journal of Pharmaceutical Policy and Practice
    • Key Points:
      • FDCs are combinations of two or more Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) in a fixed dose.
      • FDCs are linked to issues like Anti-Microbial Resistance (AMR).
      • They are used for improving patient compliance, notably in AIDS treatment.
      • Concerns include reduced therapeutic efficacy.
      • Drug Price Control Order (DPCO) does not cover drug combinations.
      • No standardization by the Indian Pharmacopoeia for these drugs.
      • Common combinations:
        • Anti-inflammatory drugs with vitamins.
        • Antihistamines with anti-diarrhea medications.
        • Analgesics with sulphonamides.
      • FDCs can be priced higher, leading to economic concerns.
      • Need for reforms in the Drug and Cosmetics Act of 1940, considered outdated.
      • Central Drug Standard Control Organisation, led by the Drugs Controller General of India, oversees these matters.
      • Statistics:
        • 60.5% of FDCs in India are unapproved.
        • 9.9% of FDCs are sold despite being banned
