  • CN

    1. Schedule 7

      1. Water, Irrigation and Use of Water is Entry 17 of the State List

      2. But Entry 17 is Subject to Provisions of Entry 56 of Union List. Entry 56 deals with Inter State River Waters

        This Creates an Inherent Problem of Use v/s Regulations

    2. Art 262

      1. Adjudication of Water Disputes, Parliament has the power to make Laws for settling River Water Disputes.

      2. There is a Constitutional Exception and Uniqueness for Courts including Supreme Court

        Courts including SC can be kept out if parliament passes a law to that effect

  • Laws

    1. Based on the Art 262 of the constitution, Parliament enacted Inter State River Water Disputes Act 1956 was enacted which provides for the Tribunal of River Water Tribunals for the Adjudication of River Water Disputes
    2. Parliament Also Passed the River Boards Act 1956 to enable Union Government to create rivwer boats for Inter State Rivers, which would advise on the preparation of various development schemes and prevent emergence of conflicts. However No River Boards has been created under this particular law till date
    3. In 2019, Inter State River Water Amendment Bill is Introduced. ISRW(A) Bill Act 2019

          When New States were formed water resources were not fairly divided

        1. Unclear Lines of Power and Responsibilities between Centre & State. Which is continuation of Government of India Act, 1935. This creates Jurisdictional Ambiguities.

        but SC can take decision on State and Centre and State and State Dispute but under SLP of Exrra Ordinary Judicial Power it allows supreme court to hear appeals against verdicts or awards given by High Courts or Tribunals.

  • ISRW D B 2019

    Key Provisions

    1. Under Present ICRW D Act - State can Approach Centre → Centre will constitute a River Water Tribunal. Hence Presently you have to Approach Centre and there are Multiple Tribunals which are Ad Hoc in Nature

      In the New Bill. Dispute Resolution Mechanism will have time period

      1. Tier 1 → Dispute Resolution Committee which will be headed by a Secretary Level Officer of Central Government it can take up around 18 months to try to find a Solution. If it is not able to resolve within the allocated time, then it will go to Tier 2

      2. Tier 2 → Here there is a Permanent Standing Tribunal Body needs to resolve the issue within 3 Years and then if it wants it can reoslve within another 18 months

        Hence in Total 3 Years or 36 Months

    2. There will also be River Basin wise Data. There should be real and better data needs to be collected over simple Arithematical Hydrology

  • Dont Know What It is

  • Way Forward

    According to Dr Srinivas Chokkakula, He Reccomended a

    1. Revised National Water Policy from 1987 to 2002 and now a new revision is needed which will lay down principals and objectives for the management development and regulation for water resources

    2. Reccomended that Central Government needs to Redefine its Role in Central Government to Facilitate Inter State Cooperation - Govt Backing of a Policy

    3. There should be a role for Inter State Council for Solving this kind of Federal Disputes

      He Says that Art 263 (Inter State Council) comes after Art 262 (ISRW D Adjudication). Hence Constitutional makers say that ISC which is Art 263 should have a role in it

    4. River Basin Mgmt for Sustainble Management of River Basins

    5. There should be more Conensus based Negotiation and Reoslution rather than Adversial Based Models which calls for Delay

      Australlia has a Sophesticated Water Market Model which allows for Trading of Water
