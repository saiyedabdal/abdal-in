        • Town Planning

          Image - Lower Town & Upper Town

          Generally, the Indus towns were divided into two parts ie Upper town and lower town

          • Upper Town
            1. Upper town was created on an upraised platform
            2. It was commonly known as Citadel - In this part of the town administrative buildings were created
            3. It also included granaries for the storage of grains
            4. Another notable structure in the upper town was the common bath meant for ritual bathing
            5. For Example: the Great Bath of Mohenjodaro in which stairs were created in North and South Direction
            6. Moreover, stairs were only made up of burnt bricks
            7. Generally, the upper town was mainly fortified ie had boundary walls
          • Lower Turn
            1. The lower town was the biggest part meant for the habitation of commoners
            2. It was divided into many sectors
            3. There was a very good network of roads and these roads used to cut each other at right angles
            4. On both the sides of roads, drains were created which were covered with stone or brick slabs
            5. Drains had the provision of the man-hole for easy cleaning of drain and groundwater recharge

            4. All the houses were connected with the main drain, etc

  • Classical Music - by Vision IAS

    • Indian Music
      • The earliest reference to music can be found in Rig Veda
      • All those Hymn's of Rig Veda which were sung constitute Sam Veda
      • Sam Veda is considered as the Veda of Music and it mentions near about 16000 Raga and Ragini (Melodies)
      • There used to be a special priest in the Vedic period who used to sing the Hymns and he was known as Udgatar.
      • The later part of Samveda is known as Gandharvaveda which is considered as the rule book of Music
      • It mentions Nada or Sound as the basis of creation and refers to OM as the first universal sound
    • Basics of the Indian Music
      • Swara
        1. Indian Music is considered as Heptatonic, whereby there are 7 pure notes, tone or frequency in Indian Music
        2. Each Swara is made up of many shrutis
        3. Shrutis is the feebles sound which human ear can perceive
        4. The seven pure swaras of Indian Music:
          1. Sa- Sadja
          2. Re - Reshabha
          3. Ga - Gandhara
          4. Ma - Madhyam
          5. Pa - Pancham
          6. Dha - Dhavaita
          7. Ni -Nishada
      • Raga
        1. It is the basis of melody
        2. It is the combination of different swaras
        3. To create a Raga, there must be at least 5 distinct notes
        4. On the basis of distinct notes, three types of Raga can be created:
          1. Odhva Raga - 5 distinct Raga
          2. Sadhva Raga - 6 distinct notes
          3. Sampoorna Raga - All the 7 distinct notes
      • Tala
        1. It is the basis of rhythm
        2. It is the arrangement of beats in a cyclical manner
        3. The most popular rhythmic cycles in India are as follows -
          1. Thisra (3 rhythmic cycles)
          2. Chatusra (4 rhythmic cycles)
          3. Khanda (5 rhythmic cycles)
          4. Misra (6 rhythmic cycles)
          5. Sankirtana (7 rhythmic cycles)
    • Hindustani Music
      1. Initially, there was no difference between North and South Indian Music
      2. The differentiation started in the Medieval times
      3. Moreover, in the Medieval period, the singers of the north started to follow the time, season and mood theory of Raga
      4. After which it was completely branched-off from South Indian Music
      5. In the beginning, the 6 principal ragas were created which are as follows:
        1. Raga - Time - Season - Mood:
        2. Bhairavi - Dawn - Spring - Awe/Devotion
        3. Shri - Evening - Winter - Love and Gladness
        4. Megha - Dawn - Rainy - Courage
        5. Deepak - Afternoon -Summer - Compassion
        6. Hindola - Early Morning - Spring - Sweetness of Young Couple
        7. Malkauns - Midnight - Winter - Laughter
    • Different Composition of Hindustani Music
      • Classical Versions
        1. Dhrupad - It is the oldest classical music of India whose origin can be traced back to the Vedic period
        2. Dhrupad is a simple and sober composition which demands efforts from lungs and vocal cords
        3. Dhrupad performance starts with an alap followed by a Jod (interconnector), then followed by 4 composed sections which are Sthayi, Antara, Sanchari and Abhog
        4. Generally, the Dhrupad lyrics were composed in Braj and Awadhi lyrics
        5. There are two musical instruments - Tanpura and Pakhawaj which is always accompanied in Dhrupad Music
        6. Raja Man Singh Tomar of Gwalior popularised Dhrupad
        7. There were many famous dhrupad singers in the Akbar's court which included Baiju Bawra. Guru Gopal Das and Tansen
        8. Tansen was the most accomplished Dhrupad singer of that time
        9. His original name was Ram Tanu Pandey and he was trained in Dhrupad by his guru Swami Haridas
        10. Akbar gave him the title of Miyan ie Master
        11. Gharana - The creation of different Gharanas in Music depends upon the aptitude of Music, Temperament and Dialect

        1. Dagar Gharana
        2. Bettiah Gharana
        3. Darbhanga Gharana
        4. Bishnupur Gharana

        1. Khayal is a Persian word that means thought or imagination
        2. The origin of Khayal can be attributed to Amir Khusro
        3. It is based on romantic raga
        4. Unlike Dhrupad, Khayal has much more freedom in structure and form of singing
        5. But singers must have to emphasize the thought that they are representing in the music
        6. Taan is the special feature of Khayal Music
        7. Khayal performance takes place in two stages - Chota Khayal and Bada Khayal
        8. The famous Gharanas of Khayal - Kairana Gharana; Gwalior Gharana; Jaipur Gharana; Patiala Gharana and Agra Gharana

    • Abugida System

      1. Vowels and consonants.
      2. Consonants are fully written
      3. Vowels are diacritic (not written fully)
      4. Indian languages are considered as one of the most advanced languages in the world
      5. Indian languages follow the Abugida system of writing
      6. As per this system, the written words correspond to the diction/pronunciation
      7. In the Abugida system, consonants are always written, and vowels are used as diacritics
      8. The only exception is when any word is going to start from a vowel, then it is fully written

      • Introduction to Sanskrit Literature
        1. Sanskrit was not born in India but it flourished in India, with the arrival of Aryans in India.

      The Four Vedas

      • Rig Veda
        1. Rig Veda is the earliest collection of 1028 hymns.
        2. On the basis of themes, it can be divided into three parts.
        3. First part: It gives beautiful descriptions of nature, like mountains, rivers, etc.
        4. Second part: It is also a book of prayers and mantras which talks about the development of highly cultured society.
        5. Third part: it is an important source of information about early vedic, polity, society and economy.
        6. Hotra Priest was considered as an expert of Rigveda.
      • Samaveda
        1. All those hymns of Rigveda which were sung constitute Samveda
        2. Saman means music
        3. There are total 1549 mantras out of which only 75 were newly composed and rest belonged to Rigveda
        4. The later part of Sama Veda is known as Gandharva Veda
        5. Gandharva Veda is considered as the Rule Book of music
        6. The expert priest of Sama Veda is known as Udgatar
      • Yajurveda
        1. It was the most popular of all Vedas
        2. Which is associated with the rituals and sacrifices
        3. It provides guidelines to the performance of yajna
        4. Yajurveda is divided into 40 chapters
        5. The priest who was expert of Yajurveda rituals was known as Adhvaryu
        6. There are two branches of Yajurveda: Krishna Yajurveda and Shukla Yajurveda
      • Atharvaveda
        1. The origin of this Atharvaveda can be traced to two Rishis, Atharva and Angira
        2. That is why it is also mentioned as Atharvarasa, and Angira rasa
        3. The expert of Atharva Veda is known as Brahma
        4. Atharva veda is the most elaborate of all Vedas
        5. Atharva veda consists of charms and magic
        6. It also mentions the cure of 99 diseases
        7. It is also a book of Mathematics and Astrology
